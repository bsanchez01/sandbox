Key Insights & Analysis
Data-driven observations for academic leadership decision-making
36.4%
Marshallese Studies — Lowest Department Pass Rate
Marshallese Studies (MS) posted only a 36.4% pass rate across 11 enrollments — the lowest of all departments. Spring 2025 was 0%, a critical outcome. Meanwhile Liberal Arts (LA) carries the heaviest burden at 3,034 enrollments with a 63.9% pass rate.
76.9%
Workforce & Vocational — Consistent Performer
The WV department (Workforce & Vocational) achieved a 76.9% pass rate across 350 enrolled students with near-perfect completion. Performance held strong across all three semesters (76.1% Fall → 77.6% Spring → 77.8% Summer), signalling stable instructional quality.
+13.6pp
Summer 2025 Surge Across All Departments
Every department that operated in Summer 2025 outperformed Fall and Spring. Education jumped from 72.6% (Fall) to 85.2% (Summer). Liberal Arts rose from 61.1% (Fall) to 78.7% (Summer). Nursing improved from 62.8% to 75.0%. Summer's cohort selection and structure are key drivers.
3,029
Liberal Arts Carries the Largest Student Load
LA (Liberal Arts) enrolled 3,034 students — 36% of all records — yet achieved only a 63.9% pass rate. With 1,074 failures, improving LA outcomes would have the single greatest institutional impact of any intervention.
32.3%
System-Wide Fail Rate Driven by Two Departments
Liberal Arts (34.7% fail) and Business Studies (35.6% fail) together account for over 5,000 enrollments. These two departments alone generate ~1,750 failures — more than 65% of the institution's total. Structural interventions here would decisively shift the headline metric.
75.2%
Education Department — Consistently Strong
ED (Education) maintained pass rates of 72.6% (Fall), 74.8% (Spring), and 85.2% (Summer) across 2,013 enrollments — the strongest high-volume department. The consistent upward trajectory through the year makes ED a model for sustained instructional excellence.

Actionable Recommendations
Five priority actions for academic leadership — ordered by estimated institutional impact
01
Curriculum & Instruction
Targeted Intervention for Liberal Arts — Redesign High-Failure Core Courses
With 3,029 enrollments and 1,031 failures, LA generates more than one-third of all institutional failures. ENG 097, ENG 086, ENG 096, CMI 101, and ICS 101 are the primary contributors. A co-requisite support model, integrated academic coaching from week one, and revised assessment design in these courses would move the institutional needle more than any other single action.
02
Department Review
Emergency Academic Review for MS (Marshallese Studies) and ST (Science & Technology)
MS (Marshallese Studies) posted a 36.4% pass rate overall — dropping to 0% in Spring 2025. ST (Science & Technology) fell to 37.5% in Spring. Both require immediate course-level review: curriculum audit, assessment redesign, prerequisite alignment, and faculty support assignments before the next semester offering.
03
Faculty Development
Structured Peer-Mentorship Linking WV & ED High Performers to Struggling Departments
Workforce & Vocational (82.4%) and Education (75.2%) faculty are the institution's clearest success models. Facilitate structured cross-departmental knowledge sharing — peer classroom observations, co-designed rubrics, shared early-warning frameworks — between high-performing instructors and those in MS, LA, and BS where pass rates are lowest. This leverages existing internal expertise at no additional cost.
04
Policy & Scheduling
Study and Replicate the Summer 2025 Department Performance Model
Every department improved in Summer 2025. A structured analysis comparing Summer course mix, enrollment sizes, faculty assignments, and student demographics against Fall and Spring will identify whether smaller cohorts, specific course offerings, or faculty patterns are driving gains. Apply these findings to Fall 2025 scheduling — particularly for LA, BS, and TB which have the most to gain.
05
Governance
Embed Department-Level Pass Rate Targets into Annual Governance Accountability Cycle
Set formal pass rate floors per department (e.g., ≥65% institutional minimum) and require Department Heads to present end-of-semester performance reports with variance explanations to the Academic Affairs Committee. Departments below threshold for two consecutive semesters should trigger a formal improvement plan. This dashboard should be presented at the start of each semester as a live decision-support tool.
